GT Writing Task 1 Tips

How to Score Higher in IELTS General Writing Task 1

Expert strategies for formal, semi-formal and informal IELTS letters.

Identify the Letter Type First

Before writing, determine whether the letter is formal (unknown recipient), semi-formal (known in a professional context) or informal (friend/family). The wrong tone is the most common IELTS GT Task 1 mistake and heavily penalises your Task Achievement score.

Correct Salutation & Closing

Formal: Dear Sir/Madam → Yours faithfully. Semi-formal: Dear Mr/Ms [surname] → Yours sincerely. Informal: Dear [first name] → Best wishes / Love. Using the wrong opening or closing loses marks in IELTS informal and formal letter practice.

Cover All Three Bullet Points

Every IELTS GT Writing Task 1 prompt has three bullet points. You must address all three clearly and fully. Spending too much on one and neglecting another is heavily penalised under Task Achievement — give roughly equal space to each point.

State Your Purpose in Line 1

Your opening sentence must state clearly why you are writing — e.g. "I am writing to apply for..." or "I am writing regarding a problem with...". Examiners assess your ability to communicate purpose directly. Never open a formal letter with "I want to...".

Spend Only 20 Minutes

IELTS GT Writing Task 1 is worth one third of your writing score. Spending more than 20 minutes leaves insufficient time for Task 2, which is worth twice as much. Aim for 170–190 words — enough to fully cover the task without over-writing.

Vary Linking & Vocabulary

Use paragraph connectors like "Regarding...", "Furthermore," and "I would be grateful if..." in formal letters. In informal letters, use natural connectors: "Anyway,", "By the way,", "I just wanted to let you know that...". Varied vocabulary across all letter types improves your Lexical Resource score.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything students ask about IELTS General Training Writing Task 1

What is IELTS General Training Writing Task 1?
IELTS GT Writing Task 1 requires you to write a letter of at least 150 words in 20 minutes. The letter addresses a situation described in the prompt and includes three bullet points that must all be covered. The letter may be formal, semi-formal or informal. It is worth one third of your Writing band score; Task 2 is worth the remaining two thirds.
What are the three types of IELTS GT letters?
Formal letters are written to someone you do not know personally — a manager, landlord, director or newspaper editor. Use Dear Sir/Madam, formal vocabulary, no contractions, and close with Yours faithfully. Semi-formal letters are to someone you know in a professional context — a tutor, supervisor or accommodation officer. Use Dear Mr/Ms [surname] and close with Yours sincerely. Informal letters are to friends or family. Use Dear [first name], conversational tone, contractions, and close with Best wishes or Love.
How many words should I write in IELTS GT Writing Task 1?
You must write a minimum of 150 words. Writing under 150 words results in a Task Achievement penalty. Most Band 7+ responses are between 170 and 200 words. Do not write significantly more — it wastes time needed for Task 2, which carries twice the marks.
How is IELTS GT Writing Task 1 scored?
IELTS GT Writing Task 1 is assessed across four criteria, each worth 25%: Task Achievement (did you cover all three bullet points with appropriate tone?), Coherence and Cohesion (is the letter logically organised with clear paragraph flow?), Lexical Resource (do you use varied and appropriate vocabulary?), and Grammatical Range and Accuracy (do you use varied, correct grammar?). All four are evaluated in our detailed feedback.
What is the difference between 'Yours faithfully' and 'Yours sincerely'?
Use Yours faithfully when you opened with Dear Sir/Madam (you do not know the person's name). Use Yours sincerely when you opened with Dear Mr/Ms [surname] (you know their name). Using the wrong closing is a register error that affects your Task Achievement and Lexical Resource scores in IELTS formal letter writing.

About IELTS General Training Writing Task 1 — Letter Practice

The IELTS General Training Writing Task 1 requires candidates to write a letter of at least 150 words in 20 minutes. The letter responds to a given situation and must address all three bullet points in the prompt. Unlike Academic Task 1 (which requires describing data), IELTS GT Task 1 tests your ability to communicate in writing across different social and professional contexts — the three letter types being formal (e.g. complaint, job application, official request), semi-formal (e.g. letter to a tutor, landlord or course provider you know), and informal (e.g. letter to a friend about a holiday, lost item or personal news). Register accuracy — using the correct tone, vocabulary and salutation for each letter type — is the most commonly penalised error under Task Achievement.
