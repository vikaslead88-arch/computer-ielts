Writing Task 2 Tips

How to Score Higher in IELTS Writing Task 2

Expert strategies to improve your band score across all four criteria.

Plan Before You Write

Spend the first 3–4 minutes planning your essay. Identify your position, two main body paragraph ideas, and key supporting examples. A clear plan prevents going off-topic — the biggest cause of Task Response penalties.

Introduction Formula

Paraphrase the question statement in your own words, then give a clear thesis sentence stating your overall position. Never copy the question — examiners do not count copied text toward your 250-word minimum.

Use Cohesive Devices Accurately

Linking words must be used correctly and varied — not repeated. Use discourse markers like "Furthermore", "Consequently", "In contrast" and "This is because" to connect ideas logically across paragraphs.

Upgrade Your Vocabulary

Replace common words: use substantial instead of "big", detrimental instead of "bad", mitigate instead of "reduce". Lexical Resource is 25% of your score — academic alternatives make a significant difference.

Vary Your Grammar Structures

Use a mix of simple, compound and complex sentences. Passive voice, conditionals, relative clauses and nominalisations all demonstrate Grammatical Range — one of the four Band 7+ criteria you must satisfy.

Always Write a Conclusion

An essay without a conclusion loses marks for Task Response and Coherence. Your conclusion should restate your position and summarise the main arguments — two to three sentences is enough. Never introduce new ideas here.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything students ask about IELTS Writing Task 2

What is IELTS Writing Task 2?
IELTS Writing Task 2 requires you to write an essay of at least 250 words in 40 minutes responding to a point of view, argument or problem. It is worth twice as much as Task 1 in your final Writing band score. The four assessment criteria — Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range & Accuracy — each contribute 25% to your score.
What are the four types of IELTS Writing Task 2 essays?
The four main types are: Opinion Essay (do you agree or disagree?), Discussion Essay (discuss both views and give your opinion), Advantages and Disadvantages Essay (do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?), and Problem and Solution Essay (what are the causes/problems and what solutions can you suggest?). All four types are covered across our 50 practice tests.
How many words should I write in IELTS Writing Task 2?
You must write a minimum of 250 words. Writing under 250 words will result in an automatic penalty to your Task Response score. Most Band 7+ responses are between 270 and 320 words. Writing significantly more than 320 words is not recommended as it increases the risk of grammar and coherence errors without adding to your score.
How is IELTS Writing Task 2 scored?
IELTS Writing Task 2 is assessed across four criteria, each worth 25%: Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. All four are covered in our detailed evaluation feedback.
How do I get a Band 7 in IELTS Writing Task 2?
To achieve Band 7, your essay must: fully address all parts of the task with a clear position, use a range of cohesive devices accurately, demonstrate a wide vocabulary with only occasional errors, and use a variety of complex grammatical structures with few mistakes. Consistent timed practice with detailed examiner-style feedback is the fastest way to reach Band 7.
