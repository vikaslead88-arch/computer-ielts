FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about IELTS CBT and our free practice platform.

What is the IELTS Computer-Based Test (CBT)?
The IELTS Computer-Based Test (CBT) is a version of the IELTS exam taken on a computer instead of paper. It has the same content, scoring, and structure as the paper test, but you type your writing answers, use headphones for listening, and read passages on screen. Our platform simulates this exactly so you are fully prepared.
Is the CBT IELTS easier than paper-based IELTS?
Neither is easier or harder — both have identical content and are scored the same way. However, many candidates find CBT more comfortable because you can type faster, edit easily, and highlight text on screen. The key is consistent practice — which is exactly what we offer.
Are your mock tests completely free?
Yes — 100% free, no login, no payment, no hidden fees. Every test on Computer IELTS is free to use. You get the full CBT experience including split-screen layout, auto-timer, instant band score, and detailed answer review — all at zero cost.
What is the difference between Academic and General IELTS?
Academic IELTS is required for undergraduate/postgraduate study at universities and professional registration. General Training IELTS is for work experience, training programmes, and migration to countries like Canada, Australia, and the UK. Both tests share the same Listening, Speaking, and Writing Task 2.
How can I improve my IELTS band score quickly?
The fastest way to improve: (1) Take full timed mock tests regularly. (2) Review every wrong answer carefully. (3) Build academic vocabulary daily. (4) Practice skimming and scanning for reading. (5) For writing, study band descriptors and get AI feedback. (6) Speak aloud every day for Speaking. Consistent daily practice over 4–8 weeks is the most reliable path to Band 7+.
How to get Band 7 or above in IELTS?
To achieve Band 7+: Reading — aim for 30+ correct answers. Listening — aim for 30+ and focus on accuracy and spelling. Writing — use a range of vocabulary, complex grammar, and respond directly to the question. Speaking — speak fluently with minimal hesitation and develop your ideas fully.
